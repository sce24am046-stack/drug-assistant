Delaney ESOL Model (Estimating Solubility Directly from Molecular Structure)

Aqueous solubility is a critical physicochemical property in modern drug discovery. Poor water solubility frequently leads to low bioavailability, erratic oral absorption, and attrition in preclinical pipelines.

LogS = 0.16 - 0.63(cLogP) - 0.0062(MW) + 0.066(Rotatable Bonds) - 0.74(Aromatic Proportion)

Parameter Roles & Mechanics:

  • cLogP (Octanol-Water Partition): Hydrophobicity penalty. High lipophilicity directly hinders hydration by water molecules.
  • Molecular Weight (MW): Cavity formation penalty. Larger molecules require more free energy to disrupt the water hydrogen-bonding network.
  • Rotatable Bonds (RB): Conformational entropy bonus. Greater molecular flexibility improves dissolution dynamics.
  • Aromatic Proportion (AP): Lattice energy penalty. Planar aromatic rings form dense crystal π-π stacking lattices that strongly resist dissolution.

Literature References & Benchmarks:

  2. Lipinski, C. A., et al. (1997). Experimental and computational approaches to estimate solubility and permeability in drug discovery and development settings. Advanced Drug Delivery Reviews, 23(1-3), 3–25.
  3. Sorkun, M. C., et al. (2019). AqSolDB, a curated aqueous solubility dataset of chemical compounds. Scientific Data, 6(1), 143.
